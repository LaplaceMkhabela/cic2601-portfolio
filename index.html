My Teaching Philosophy

As an educator, I believe that teaching is not merely the transmission of knowledge but the cultivation of curiosity, critical thinking, and a lifelong love for learning. My teaching philosophy is grounded in the constructivist approach, where learners actively construct meaning through experience, collaboration, and reflection.

I am committed to creating an inclusive classroom environment where every learner feels valued and capable of success. I recognise that learners come with diverse backgrounds, abilities, and learning styles, and I strive to differentiate instruction so that these varied needs are met.

By integrating Information and Communication Technology (ICT), I aim to make learning relevant, engaging, and accessible to all learners. Technology is not an add-on but a powerful tool that can enhance teaching, learning, and assessment when used thoughtfully and purposefully.

ICT Integration Philosophy

I view ICT integration in Geography as an opportunity to transform abstract concepts into tangible learning experiences. I believe in using technology to:

  • Engage learners through interactive and visually rich content
  • Enable collaboration through digital tools and platforms
  • Enhance understanding through data visualisation and real-world applications
  • Empower learners to become creators, not consumers, of knowledge

Lesson Plan 1: Geography (ICT-Enhanced)

Population Growth and Change — Digital Infographic

CAPS-aligned lesson using Canva, Google Jamboard and Google Forms to explore population concepts, data interpretation and digital presentation.

Duration60 minutes

CAPS ALIGNMENT

This lesson aligns with the CAPS curriculum for Grade 7 Geography, Term 3, which focuses on “Population Growth and Change.” The curriculum requires learners to understand key population concepts, interpret population data, and analyse factors influencing population growth.

Lesson Objectives (Bloom’s Taxonomy)
TitleLesson Objective
RememberingBy the end of the lesson, learners will be able to define the key population concepts: population, birth rate, death rate, infant mortality, and population growth rate.
UnderstandingBy the end of the lesson, learners will be able to explain the factors that influence birth and death rates, including diseases, healthcare, and economic conditions.
ApplyingBy the end of the lesson, learners will be able to analyse population data and create a digital infographic showing population trends using ICT tools (Canva).

RESOURCES NEEDED

  • Laptops/tablets with internet access
  • Interactive whiteboard
  • Google Jamboard access
  • Google Docs / Google Classroom
  • Canva accounts (free)
  • Google Forms
  • YouTube video: “World Population Growth” (2–3 minutes)
  • Research links: Stats SA, World Bank, World Population Review

LESSON PHASES

Introduction Phase (10 minutes)

  • The teacher displays a large KWL chart using Google Jamboard.
  • The teacher shows a brief YouTube video (2–3 minutes) on world population growth.
  • The teacher asks: “What do you already know about population? Why do you think populations grow or decline?”
  • Learners contribute ideas to the class KWL chart.
  • Learners complete their own KWL chart on Google Docs.
  • The teacher explains the lesson objectives.

Development Phase (25 minutes)

  • The teacher provides research links and questions.
  • The teacher demonstrates how to use Canva’s infographic templates.
  • Learners work in pairs to research population data for South Africa.
  • Learners create a digital infographic on Canva including:
    • South Africa’s current population
    • Birth rate and death rate data
    • Population growth rate
    • Factors influencing birth/death rates
  • The teacher circulates to provide support and guidance.

Consolidation Phase (10 minutes)

  • Learners complete an online quiz on Google Forms.
  • Learners receive immediate feedback on their answers.
  • Learners return to their KWL charts and complete the “L” column.
  • The teacher reviews key concepts and addresses any misconceptions.

ASSESSMENT

  • Formative: Class participation, infographic quality, quiz results
  • Infographic Rubric: Accuracy of data, clarity of explanations, design quality, use of graphics

EXTENSION ACTIVITIES

  • Research population data for multiple countries and compare trends
  • Investigate the impact of HIV/AIDS on South Africa’s population growth
  • Create an animated timeline showing world population growth
Lesson Plan 2: Geography

Population Growth and Change

Meaning of population growth and population change; causes; interpreting population data; effects on community services.

Duration60 minutes
Lesson Metadata & LTSM / Resources
Name and student numberSthandiwe Mbokazi 22773886
GradeGrade 7
Lesson topic areaPopulation growth and change
Lesson sub-topicMeaning of population growth and population change; causes; interpreting population data; effects on community services.
Duration60 minutes
LTSM/resources (ICT integration) 1. Laptop showing a short slide presentation or a simple population graph.
2. Printed copy of the same slides/graph as a low-technology backup.
3. Chalkboard/whiteboard and markers/chalk.
4. Learner worksheet with a simple population table and graph.
5. Textbook and pencils/rulers.

ICT integration: use the laptop to display the graph or a short, downloaded educational visual. If internet/projector, display the material on the laptop in small groups or use printed copies.

LESSON AIMS

By the end of the lesson leaners should be able to:

  1. Define population growth and population change in their own words.
  2. Identify at least three factors that can cause population size to change, including births, deaths and migration.
  3. Interpret a simple population table or bar graph and identify whether a population is increasing or decreasing.
  4. Explain two possible effects of population growth on services such as housing, schools, water, transport or healthcare.
  5. Suggest two responsible ways a community or municipality can plan for population change.

TEACHING STRATEGY

Learner-centred, inquiry-based learning. Begin with a familiar question about how the local community has changed, then guide learners to use evidence from a table or graph. This strategy connects Geography to learners’ lived experiences and encourages observation, questioning and problem-solving rather than memorising definitions only.

TEACHING METHOD

Question-and-answer discussion combined with explicit explanation and guided graph interpretation. The teacher models how to read the title, labels and values on a graph, then learners practise in pairs. This method is suitable because learners need both clear explanations of key terms and practical opportunities to interpret population information.

LESSON PHASES
Phase/timeTeacher activitiesLearner activities
Introduction
(10 minutes)		 Display two images or ask learners to imagine the local area several years ago and today. Ask: “Have you noticed more houses, shops, cars or people in our community?” Record responses. Introduce the lesson question: “Why do populations change, and what effects can this have?” Share observations from their community, listen to classmates and suggest reasons why the number of people may have changed.
Development
(35 minutes)		 Explain population growth (an increase in the number of people over time) and population change (a change in population size or characteristics). Introduce births, deaths, immigration and emigration. Display a simple example table: Community A 2020: 1,000 people; 2025: 1,250 people. Model how to compare values and describe the change. Discuss effects such as demand for homes, classrooms, water, jobs and clinics, while noting that planning can help communities respond. Give pairs a worksheet with a small table/bar graph and guide them with questions. Copy key terms, answer oral questions and work in pairs to read the graph/table. Identify whether the population increased or decreased, support answers with figures and discuss possible effects on local services.
Consolidation
(15 minutes)		 Ask learners to complete a short exit task: define population growth, name two causes of population change, state what the sample data show and suggest one service the community may need to plan for. Review answers and correct misunderstandings. Complete the exit task independently, share one answer and correct their notes if needed.

ASSESSMENT DURING THE LESSON

1. What is population growth?

Possible answer: An increase in the number of people in a place over time.

2. Name two factors that change population size.

Possible answer: Births and deaths; immigration and emigration are also correct.

3. The example population increased from 1,000 in 2020 to 1,250 in 2025. Did it grow or decline?

Possible answer: It grew.

4. Name one service that may experience greater demand when a community grows.

Possible answer: Schools, housing, water, transport or healthcare.

5. Why should local authorities understand population change?

Possible answer: To plan services and resources for the community.

Assessment evidence: oral responses, pair worksheet and individual exit task.

HOMEWORK (connected to real life)

  1. Ask a family member or trusted community member how the local area has changed over the past five to ten years.
  2. Write down two changes they describe (for example, new houses, more people or new services).
  3. Suggest one possible reason for these changes, making clear if it is your own suggestion rather than a confirmed fact.
  4. Identify one local service that may need to grow or improve if the population increases.
  5. Write a short paragraph (5-7 sentences) explaining why understanding population growth and change matters to your community. Do not collect anyone’s private information.

REFLECTION (complete after presenting the lesson)

What went well?

I explained population growth using examples that learners could relate to, such as the number of people living in their communities. Pictures and questions helped learners understand the topic. Some learners participated actively by sharing their ideas.

What challenges did I experience?

Some learners struggled to understand the difference between birth rates, death rates and population growth. I also noticed that some learners were shy to answer questions.

What could I improve?

I could use more diagrams, pictures and simple examples to explain population growth. I would also give learners more opportunities to discuss answers in pairs before sharing them with the class.

What did I learn?

I learned that Geography becomes easier to understand when I connect the content to learners’ everyday experiences. I also learned that I need to check learners’ understanding throughout the lesson.

Lesson Plan 3: History

The colonisation of the Cape in the 17th and 18th centuries

The arrival of the Dutch East India Company in 1652, reasons for the refreshment station, expansion of settlement and effects on the Khoikhoi and San.

Duration60 minutes
Lesson Metadata & LTSM / Resources
Name and student numberSthandiwe Mbokazi 22773886
GradeGrade 7
Lesson topic areaThe colonisation of the Cape in the 17th and 18th centuries
Lesson sub-topicThe arrival of the Dutch East India Company in 1652, reasons for the refreshment station, expansion of settlement and effects on the Khoikhoi and San.
Duration60 minutes
LTSM/resources (ICT integration) 1. Laptop showing a map of the Cape and a short timeline or source images.
2. Printed map/timeline and image
3. Chalkboard/whiteboard and markers/chalk.
4. Learner worksheet with timeline events and source questions.
5. Grade 7 Social Sciences textbook
Projector.

ICT integration: use a laptop to display the map and historical images.

LESSON AIMS

At the end of the lesson leaners should be able to:

  1. Define colonisation and identify the Cape as the place where Dutch settlement began in 1652.
  2. Place 1652 and selected events of the 17th and 18th centuries in chronological order using a simple timeline.
  3. Explain why the Dutch East India Company established a refreshment station at the Cape.
  4. Describe at least two ways Dutch colonisation affected the Khoikhoi and San communities.
  5. Use a historical source or image to distinguish between a historical fact and a question that needs further evidence.

TEACHING STRATEGY

Source based and enquiry learning. Learners examine a map, timeline and selected images, then ask what the sources show and whose perspectives may be represented or missing. This strategy supports historical thinking and avoids presenting colonisation only from the settlers’ point of view.

TEACHING METHOD

Storytelling/teacher explanation supported by guided source analysis, questioning and pair work. The teacher gives the historical context and models how to read a source; learners then sequence events and discuss impacts. This method makes the period understandable while helping learners consider the experiences of the Khoikhoi and San.

LESSON PHASES
Phase/timeTeacher activitiesLearner activities
Introduction
(10 minutes)		 Show a map locating the Cape at the southern tip of Africa and a picture of Table Bay. Ask learners what they know about the Cape before and after European settlement. Explain that the lesson examines Dutch colonisation during the 17th and 18th centuries and its effects on people already living there. Locate the Cape on the map, share prior knowledge and ask questions about who lived in the region before Dutch settlement.
Development
(35 minutes)		 Explain that in 1652 the Dutch East India Company (VOC) established a refreshment station at the Cape to supply ships travelling between Europe and Asia. Use a timeline to place 1652 and later developments in the 17th and 18th centuries. Explain that settlement expanded over time. Discuss effects on Khoikhoi and San communities, including loss of access to land and grazing areas, pressure on livelihoods, unequal power relations, conflict and the spread of diseases. Explain that experiences differed between communities and individuals. Display a source image/map and ask learners what it shows and what it cannot tell us on its own. Take brief notes, help place events on the timeline, examine the source in pairs and identify at least two effects on Khoikhoi and San communities. Share ideas respectfully and recognise that colonisation affected different groups differently.
Consolidation
(15 minutes)		 Ask learners to complete a three-part exit ticket: one fact about 1652, one reason the VOC established the station, and one effect of colonisation on indigenous communities. Review answers and correct any confusion between the VOC’s initial purpose and later expansion of settlement. Complete the exit ticket independently and explain one thing they learned about the effects of colonisation.

ASSESSMENT DURING THE LESSON

1. What does colonisation mean?

Possible answer: When a country or outside power establishes control or settlements in another area and its people.

2. In which year did the Dutch East India Company establish a refreshment station at the Cape?

Possible answer: 1652.

3. Why was the refreshment station established?

Possible answer: To provide fresh food, water and supplies to ships travelling between Europe and Asia.

4. Name one way colonisation affected the Khoikhoi or San.

Possible answer: They lost access to land or grazing, experienced conflict, or faced disruption to their livelihoods.

5. Why should historians use maps, images and written sources carefully?

Possible answer: Sources provide evidence but may show only one perspective or leave information out.

Assessment evidence: timeline activity, pair discussion and individual exit ticket.

HOMEWORK

  1. Draw a simple timeline with 1652 marked clearly and label it as the year the VOC established a refreshment station at the Cape.
  2. Write two sentences explaining why the Cape was useful to ships travelling between Europe and Asia.
  3. Write three sentences describing two effects of colonisation on the Khoikhoi and San communities.
  4. Ask an adult or consult a reliable textbook about one place name, museum or historical site connected to early Cape history; record the source you used.
  5. Write a short paragraph explaining why it is important to learn about the experiences of the people who lived at the Cape before colonisation. Use respectful language and do not copy directly from a source.

REFLECTION

What went well?

I introduced the topic by explaining the arrival of the Dutch at the Cape. Using pictures and explaining the journey from the Netherlands to the Cape helped learners develop a better understanding of the historical events.

What challenges did I experience?

Some learners found it difficult to remember the sequence of historical events and understand how colonisation affected the Khoikhoi and San communities. And who the Dutch was.

What could I improve?

I could use a timeline and a map to show the sequence of events and the journey to the Cape. I would also ask more questions about the effects of colonisation on the indigenous communities.

What did I learn?

I learned that teaching History requires more than explaining dates and events. Learners also need to understand the experiences of different groups of people and how historical events affected their lives.

Reflection

A written reflection on the lesson, and a reflection on building this portfolio with Wix.

Introduction

This reflection is on the Grade 7 Geography lesson I designed on “Population Growth and Change” for Term 3. The lesson aimed to develop learners’ understanding of population concepts and their ability to analyse and present population data using ICT tools. This reflection examines what worked well, what challenges I faced, areas for improvement, and the impact on my teaching practice.

What Worked Well

1

Engaging Introduction

The KWL chart activity using Google Jamboard effectively activated prior knowledge and generated interest in the topic. The YouTube video was engaging and provided a context for the lesson.

2

ICT Integration

The use of Canva for the infographic creation was successful. Learners were able to manage the tools and produced visually appealing infographics that demonstrated their understanding of population concepts. Google Forms quiz provided immediate feedback.

3

Collaborative Learning

Pair work allowed learners to support one another during the research and infographic phases, and learners discussed their findings.

4

Learner Engagement

The majority of learners participated actively in class discussions and completed the infographic activity.

5

Differentiation

The activities catered to different learning styles and abilities, with extension tasks provided for advanced learners and additional support for those who needed it.

Challenges Encountered

1

Technical Difficulties

Some learners experienced Internet connectivity issues, which affected their ability to access and use online tools.

2

Time Management

The 25 minutes allocated for research and infographic creation was not sufficient. Many learners needed more time to complete their infographics with all required details.

3

Digital Literacy

Some learners struggled with Canva, particularly those with limited experience using digital tools. This slowed down their progress.

4

Data Interpretation

Some learners found it challenging to interpret population statistics and connect them to the concepts taught.

Areas for Improvement

Action 01

Pre-Lesson Preparation

Pre-teach the basics of Canva before the lesson and create a template for the infographic to support struggling learners.

Action 02

Time Allocation

Allocate at least 40 minutes for the development phase to ensure learners have adequate time for research and infographic creation.

Action 03

ICT Resource Preparation

Prepare printed resources for learners who experience connectivity issues.

Action 04

More Scaffolding

Provide more scaffolding, such as guiding questions and worked examples, for data interpretation and infographic design.

Action 05

Reach Redefinition

In future, aim to move towards the Redefinition level of SAMR by having learners publish their infographics or share their population data online with peers.

Impact on My Teaching Practice

This lesson has reinforced my commitment to integrating ICT meaningfully in the classroom. I have learned that successful ICT integration requires careful planning, adequate support, and flexibility to address challenges. Moving forward, I will:

  • Plan thoroughly for ICT-based activities.
  • Provide more scaffolding for digital tools.
  • Allow more time for ICT-based activities.
  • Prepare backup resources for connectivity issues.
  • Continue to differentiate instruction to meet diverse learner needs.
  • Strive to move beyond substitution towards transformation using the SAMR model.

Inclusive Teaching (Differentiation)

To accommodate learners with different learning abilities, I implemented the following strategies:

Visual Learners

The Canva infographic activity and visual presentation aided visual learning styles.

Kinesthetic Learners

The hands-on KWL chart creation and infographic creation promoted engagement.

Auditory Learners

The video introduction and class discussions supported auditory learning.

Advanced Learners

Extension activities, including researching multiple countries and investigating HIV/AIDS impact on population growth.

Learners Needing Support

Word banks, sentence starters, a visual glossary and simplified data sets provided additional help.

Multiple Means

Learners could demonstrate understanding through graphical, visual and written explanations, or the quiz.

Conclusion

Overall, the lesson was successful in achieving its learning objectives and engaging learners. The integration of ICT tools enhanced the learning experience and fostered important 21st-century digital literacy skills. I am committed to continuously improving my teaching practice and using technology to create meaningful and inclusive learning experiences for all learners.

Reflection on the Use of Wix

Question 01

What was your first impression of using Wix?

My first impression of Wix was that it was a useful platform for creating a website without needing advanced coding skills. At first, I needed time to understand how to arrange the pages, add content and choose suitable designs. As I continued working on my portfolio, I became more comfortable using the platform. I realised that Wix can help teachers present their work in a professional and organised way.

Question 02

Which Wix features did you find most useful and why?

I found the page and menu features useful because they allowed me to organise my portfolio into sections such as Home, Lesson Plans, Activities, Reflections, Videos and References. I also found the image and video features useful because they helped me present my teaching activities in a more interesting way. These features made it easier for visitors to find information and understand the purpose of my portfolio.

Question 03

What challenges did you encounter while building your website?

One challenge I encountered was organising the content and making sure that each item appeared under the correct page. I also needed to pay attention to the layout, font sizes, colours and placement of images. To address these challenges, I worked on the website step by step and checked the pages after making changes. This taught me that building a website requires patience, planning and attention to detail.

Question 04

How did using Wix improve your web design or digital skills?

Using Wix helped me improve my digital skills by teaching me how to create web pages, organise information, add images and videos, and arrange content in a readable format. I also learned the importance of using consistent colours, headings and layouts. These skills have increased my confidence in using technology to present educational information.

Question 05

What would you do differently if you had to redesign your website?

If I had to redesign my website, I would plan the structure and content before starting. I would prepare my lesson plans, activities, images and references in advance so that I could organise them more efficiently. I would also make sure that the website uses consistent colours and fonts, that the text is easy to read, and that all links and videos work correctly. I would ask someone to view the website and give feedback before publishing it.

Question 06

How can the skills learned from Wix be applied in your future career?

As a future teacher, I can use the skills I learned from Wix to create educational websites and organise learning resources for learners. I could use a website to share lesson materials, instructions, activities and revision resources. These skills can also help me integrate information and communication technology into my teaching. Overall, learning to use Wix has shown me how digital tools can support communication, creativity and teaching.

Computer Risks, Safeguards & Ethics: Key Takeaways

Key takeaways for educators and learners

Malware & Phishing Prevention

Implementing firewall controls, keeping antivirus software updated, and educating students on identifying suspicious download links or phishing emails.

Student Data Privacy & POPIA Compliance

Safeguarding learner personal data, secure storage of digital grades, and ensuring consent protocols when using third-party educational tools.

Copyright & Ethical Content Use

Promoting digital honesty, proper citation of internet sources, avoiding academic plagiarism, and using Creative Commons licensed media in lessons.

Cyberbullying & Digital Citizenship

Fostering respectful online communication, establishing clear classroom digital etiquette guidelines, and supporting positive digital footprints.
